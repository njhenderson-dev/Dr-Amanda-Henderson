It is not always obvious whether a problem needs a GP, urgent care, emergency department or an ambulance. Knowing roughly which service fits which problem can save time and, more importantly, keep you safe.

## Why this matters

Choosing the right level of care matters because decisions affect both time and safety. A GP can assess many problems, including infections, rashes, injuries, medication concerns, mental health issues, pregnancy-related questions and persistent symptoms. Urgent care or emergency departments become necessary when symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening, involve serious injury, or need hospital-level testing or treatment.

## A few helpful terms

- **Urgent care:** Care for problems that should be assessed soon but are not immediately life-threatening.
- **Emergency department:** Hospital care for serious or potentially life-threatening illness or injury.
- **000:** Australia's emergency number for ambulance, police or fire.

## Common reasons to book a GP appointment

- You have a mild to moderate infection, rash, or non-severe pain.
- You have medication questions or need a prescription reviewed.
- You need preventive care, screening, or follow-up after hospital care.
- You have a chronic condition flare or a mental health concern.
- You are not sure whether a symptom can wait and want it assessed.

## What we can talk through together

A GP appointment may be suitable for mild to moderate infections, skin concerns, medication questions, non-severe pain, preventive care, chronic disease flares, mental health support, referrals and follow-up after hospital care. If your GP finds that a problem needs hospital-level assessment, they can arrange referral to emergency or specialist care.

## What to expect at the appointment

When contacting a practice, it helps to describe the symptom, the patient's age, pregnancy status, severity, timing and any concerning signs. The receptionist or nurse may advise urgent care or emergency services rather than booking, and that is a safety step, not a refusal of care. If your condition deteriorates while waiting, do not drive yourself; contact emergency services instead.

## How to prepare

Before you book, think about how severe the problem is, how quickly it is changing, and whether there are any red flags. Have a medication list and relevant history ready. If you are unsure whether a problem is an emergency, it is safer to seek urgent assessment than to wait.

## When to seek urgent help

Call 000 immediately for chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, severe allergic reactions, stroke symptoms, collapse, severe bleeding, major trauma, drowning, poisoning, severe burns, prolonged seizures, a suicidal crisis with immediate danger, or any situation where life may be at immediate risk without emergency intervention.

## Common questions

### When is a GP appointment appropriate?

A GP is appropriate when the person is stable, symptoms are not life-threatening, and the issue can be assessed safely in general practice.

### What if I am not sure it is an emergency?

If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or frightening, err on the side of urgent assessment. Call 000 for life-threatening concerns.

### Can a GP send me to hospital?

Yes. A GP can assess and refer to emergency or specialist care if hospital-level assessment is needed.

### Should I wait overnight for a GP appointment?

Not if there are red flags, severe pain, breathing difficulty, dehydration, neurological symptoms, heavy bleeding, serious injury or you feel dangerously unwell.
